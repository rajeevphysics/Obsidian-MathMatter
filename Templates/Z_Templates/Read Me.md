# How To Write Concepts 
All concepts should follow the [[Template|template]] format. To make writing easier. The definition of a concept should avoid using complex jargon. If using jargon is needed, make another concept that explains that jargon, and backlink it into your definition. 

Keep in mind your concept is being used by students who likely have little knowledge of the concept. So, try to explain every assumption and predefined knowledge in your concept. 

Your concept should be understood by a reader who doesn't have any knowledge of the concept.

## Example For Definition
The integration factor is a technique for solving [[Homogeneous & Non-Homogeneous|non-homogeneous]], [[Separable & Non-Separable ODE|non-separable]] [[Order|first-order]] [[Ordinary Differential Equations (ODE's)|ODEs]]. It involves creating a replica of the ODE being solved that is [[Homogeneous & Non-Homogeneous|homogeneous]] and [[Separable & Non-Separable ODE|separable]]. Then, using solving for the variable $y$ which will be multiplied by every term in the [[Homogeneous & Non-Homogeneous|non-homogeneous]] ODE. This forces one side of the equation to collapse as a [[Differentiable|differentiable]] product, making it easier to [[Indefinite Integrals|integrate]] and solve. 

This is a definition for the integration factor in differential equations. Although readers who read this concept likely have an idea of what homogenous, integration, differentiables, etc, are, they are still backlinked where that idea is explained further (although not explained in this example)
